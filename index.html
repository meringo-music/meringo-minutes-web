    What it says

Click a timestamp. Hear it said.

Every summary point carries the moment it came from. Click it and the transcript opens on that line, with the recording cued to play from there.

Sometimes it lands a little early. Treat a timestamp as the place to start listening; the line is close.

Ask it a question. It cites the line.

Each sentence of an answer quotes the transcript lines it came from. When nothing in the meeting holds, the whole answer is I found nothing in this meeting that says that.

In the test meeting it refused all 8 questions the transcript doesn't fully answer. When it's unsure it says nothing, which is the mistake I'd rather it made.

A second model checks the first.

With Ollama, the default setup, a different model reads the summary against the transcript it was written from. A sentence it can't support moves into a folded card that says Read it as a lead, not a finding.

Set aside, not deleted: you can still open the card and read them. How it works has the detail.

It tells the room.

I'm recording this meeting to take notes. The recording stays on my Mac.

By default, that's what it says out loud as recording starts, and the words go into the recording too. You can change them, or switch it off; it stays on until you do. Nothing joins the call and no invite goes out, so the announcement is how the others find out. On headphones, the far end of a call may not hear it.

It doesn't try to work out whose consent law applies. The code gives the reason: the strictest participant's law governs, and nobody's location is knowable. It isn't legal advice.

Where a meeting goes

By default, nowhere.

Meringo Minutes keeps the recording, the transcript and the notes in one folder on your Mac, by default, and its privacy panel says so:

Audio, transcripts, summaries and mindmaps are produced and stored on this machine. There is no account, no sync and no usage tracking. The only internet use is downloading a model the first time it is needed; talking to Ollama stays on this Mac.

Point the summariser at another computer on your network and the panel turns orange and names it. A test in the build runs a real meeting, watches the network while it does, and fails on any connection that isn't local.
