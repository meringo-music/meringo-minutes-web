Where a meeting goes

Point the summariser at another computer on your network and the panel turns orange and names it. In a test for this site it read Summaries are sent to gpu-box.lan, and went on: The audio and the transcript are still only stored on this Mac.

A test in the build runs a real meeting, watches the network while it does, and fails on any connection that isn't local. It looks every 100 ms, so a connection that opens and closes between two looks would get past it.

What Meringo Minutes does

  1. Timestamps you can follow. A summary sentence's timestamp opens the transcript at that line, with the playhead parked there. In the demo, 4 of the 11 point at the wrong line, each a little early, and the right one follows within about 30 seconds.

  2. A second model, checking. With Ollama, the default setup, a second, different model reads the summary against the transcript. What it can't support moves into a folded card. It misses some; How it works shows which.

  3. Answers that cite the meeting. Ask a question and each sentence of the answer comes with the transcript lines it cites. When nothing in the meeting holds, the whole answer is I found nothing in this meeting that says that.

  4. Deadlines it won't invent. End of Q3 becomes a date. Next Friday stays in words, so it can't turn into a false Overdue.

  5. It tells the room. By default, recording starts with the app saying out loud that it's recording.

For client meetings

If you meet clients, the first thing to know is where the recording went. By default it stays in that folder on your Mac, with no account and no sync. Whether the app fits your professional obligations is your call, and this page gives you the facts I have.

By default it speaks as recording starts: I'm recording this meeting to take notes. The recording stays on my Mac. You can change the words or switch it off; it stays on until you do. The announcement goes into the recording too. One limit: on headphones, the other end of a call may not hear it.

It doesn't try to work out whose consent law applies. The code gives the reason: the strictest participant's law governs, and nobody's location is knowable. That's a design choice, not legal advice.

Nothing joins the call and no calendar invite goes out, so the spoken announcement is how the others find out.

Checking the notes is still your job. The timestamps, and the recording kept with the meeting, make it quicker.
